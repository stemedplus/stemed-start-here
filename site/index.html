Why STEMEd+?

Ours, not mine.

STEMEd+ Commons is an open, community-governed platform for STEM+ education, for everyone who teaches or studies learning, in STEM and beyond. We built it so collaboration is the norm, not competition, and what we know and learn stays open to everyone, everywhere.

Join STEMEd+See what you can do

Why we built this

Good work gets scattered, locked away, or lost

STEM+ education research and teaching happen in every discipline, but the field has never had a shared home.

Scattered

Work is spread across disciplines, societies, listservs, and journals, so people asking the same questions rarely find each other.

Locked away

Many peer-reviewed journals are held by for-profit publishers, which limits who can afford to publish and who can access the results.

Lost

Project sites, instruments, data, and teaching materials disappear when a grant ends, a lab moves, or a server is switched off.

What we're building

An open commons for STEM+ education

A place to share published and unpublished work, build sites for projects and networks, and collaborate within and across disciplines. Born from STEM education researchers who wanted to move between disciplines, to build upon rather than alongside, and to create a lasting footprint.

Open access

Anyone, anywhere can join, read, and share.

Open scholarship

Papers, posters, data, instruments, curriculum, and more all count, each with a lasting DOI. Designed around the TRUST, FAIR, and CARE principles.

Open infrastructure

Built using Knowledge Commons and GitHub software, STEMEd+ Commons is an academy-owned and governed, open-source system. It does not and will not monetize your intellectual or personal data.

Why the plus? STEMEd+ is multi-, inter-, and transdisciplinary. Every education researcher and practitioner is welcome, well beyond the traditional STEM disciplines, and so are the education-adjacent fields that study how people learn about, talk about, and live with science: learning sciences, science and health communication, history and philosophy of science, human dimensions, and more.

Why take part

What you get, and what you give

Your work gets found

Deposit papers, data, and teaching materials with a DOI, in a library built for STEM+ education, so people can find, use, and cite them.

You find your people

Profiles, groups, and the collaborator directory connect you with colleagues across disciplines who work on what you do.

You skip the upkeep

Sites, groups, and an open repository, hosted and maintained for you, with no lab website or server to keep alive.

It stays open

Run by and for the academy, not for profit. We never sell your data, and what you share stays open to read.

You help shape it

Many features started as a user’s request. A User Advisory Committee will give members a direct voice in priorities.

It grows with you

Every profile, group, and deposit makes the commons more useful for the next person who arrives.

Where we're headed

Changing how education knowledge is made and shared

Reclaiming peer review

Journals will be able to publish, review, and curate on STEMEd+ Commons, including open and collaborative review, without for-profit gatekeepers. Funded by NSF through 2028.

More humane metrics

Ways to recognize the full range of scholarly work, from datasets and instruments to curriculum and community engagement.

Communities with a say

Open science that gives communities, educators, and students a real voice in the ethical, financial, and research decisions that shape it.

Who's behind it

Built by and for the field

Funded by NSF

Grants OAC-2226271, DRL-2321946, and OAC-2531819, with support from Michigan State University’s Jenison Fund.

Built on Knowledge Commons and GitHub

Our commons (profiles, groups, sites, and the Library) is housed in Knowledge Commons, open-source software shared with the Modern Language Association, the Association of University Presses, and major universities. Our resource sites, shared menu, and automation run on GitHub, built by the STEMEd+ development team.

Guided by the community

An Advisory Board of 15+ experts in STEM education research, participatory methods, and open science, and a User Advisory Committee in formation. Led by Julie Libarkin, Director, and Linnea K. Beckett, Associate Director.

Beyond STEMEd+

Other community resources

Good work happens across the community. These are run by other organizations; we link to them rather than copy them.

  • Pathways to Science: programs, fellowships, internships, and research opportunities for students, from the Institute for Broadening Participation
  • BioQUEST and QUBES: a community for STEM education reform, with faculty mentoring networks and shared teaching resources
  • DBER Scholars Network: an interactive map of people working in discipline-based education research
  • SERC (Science Education Resource Center): teaching activities, research-based methods, and workshops for undergraduate STEM, from Carleton College
  • Lawrence Hall of Science: research-based K–12 science and math curriculum programs from UC Berkeley
  • OpenSciEd: free K–12 science curriculum aligned to the Next Generation Science Standards, developed with researchers including MSU’s Amelia Wenk Gotwals
  • PhET Interactive Simulations: free interactive math and science simulations from the University of Colorado Boulder
  • PhysPort: research-based teaching methods and assessments for physics, useful across STEM
  • InformalScience.org: an archive of informal STEM education projects, research papers, and evaluation reports
  • CourseSource: peer-reviewed, classroom-tested lessons for undergraduate biology and related fields

Collaborate with us

Write STEMEd+ into your next grant

Projects, centers, societies, and networks can partner with us through their proposals. A small budget line gives your project a lasting digital home, and helps keep the commons open for everyone.

Guided onboarding

For a very small budget line, we set up your site, public and private groups, and a curated repository collection, then train your team to run them.

Co-designed tools

Building new digital resources? Co-supervise a STEMEd+ developer who builds them with your team, inside the commons, so they outlast the grant.

A stronger proposal

A ready answer for data management, broader impacts, and sustainability: open access, DOIs, and infrastructure that stays up after funding ends.
