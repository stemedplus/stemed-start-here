Open data and feeds

Open by design

STEMEd+ Commons is built on open infrastructure, and we share what we gather the same way.

  • We don't scrape. Our job, funding, conference and other listings come from public RSS feeds and APIs, or from organizations that give us permission.
  • Everything we list, you can reuse. Each STEMEd+ site publishes its listings as RSS feeds, calendar subscriptions and JSON data files. Follow them in a feed reader, add deadlines to your calendar, or show them on your own website.
  • Your work stays findable. Work shared in the STEMEd+ Commons Library on Knowledge Commons gets a DOI and is available through OAI-PMH, the standard libraries and research services use to collect records. Each STEMEd+ collection has its own feed, in Dublin Core and DataCite formats.
  • People are identified openly. We link profiles to ORCID IDs where available and read member information through the Knowledge Commons Profiles API.
  • Our code is public. Most STEMEd+ sites are open source on GitHub, where anyone can suggest a correction or a new listing.

The STEMEd+ Commons Library: OAI-PMH

Repositories, library catalogs, and research services can collect the Library's records through OAI-PMH. The address is https://works.hcommons.org/oai2d. Add a collection's set name, as in:

https://works.hcommons.org/oai2d?verb=ListRecords&metadataPrefix=oai_dc&set=community-stemedplus

Use metadataPrefix=oai_dc for Dublin Core or oai_datacite for DataCite; MARC21 (marc21) and DCAT (dcat) are also available. A collection with no works yet answers "no records match" until something is deposited.

APIs we build on

  • Knowledge Commons Works API: the Library's records as JSON, for example works.hcommons.org/api/communities/stemedplus/records. See the KC Works API documentation.
  • STEMEd+ Commons Profiles API: members' public profiles, at stemedplus.profile.hcommons.org/api/v1/members/. Find Collaborators and the member count are built from it.

Building something with these, or need a feed we don't have? Tell us.

This site is part of STEMEd+ Commons, a Commons within Knowledge Commons. STEMEd+ Commons brings together teachers, researchers, and learning professionals from every field to share work, find each other, and build what comes next. As an open access platform, STEMEd+ Commons helps ensure STEM+ teaching and STEM+ education research are accessible for all people, in all communities.
